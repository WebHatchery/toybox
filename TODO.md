# TODO — Toybox After Hours

## Repair flow at scale

- The cross-zone scatter is **deliberate** — hunting scattered halves and rejoining them is a pillar of the game alongside sorting, and must not be shortened to make repairs cheaper. Measured on its own terms in `state/tests/replay.rs` (`Strategy::Restorer`), the errand is fully winnable: at the 240-toy shop that is all 28 broken pairs in 56 actions with nothing deferred, ~18s per pair. Cost per pair is unchanged by the shop retune; there are simply fewer pairs. The earlier "zero repairs" figure came from a nearest-first closer, which measures the sorting loop and never runs the errand at all.
- **The scanner gate is softened, not removed.** A carried half now always names the aisle its counterpart landed in ("Other half: head in Checkout"); the scanner upgrades that to distance plus the beacon column ("Scanner: head in Checkout, 17m"). Unaided the errand is a real search of one zone rather than a sweep of the whole shop for one object among hundreds, which was a wall rather than a journey. The scanner's `upgrades.json` description was rewritten to sell what it now actually adds.
- **Mistake penalty and timer are measured now, and neither number needed changing.** A wrong shelf costs 8.0s against a measured 7.8s per toy — a ratio of 1.02, i.e. exactly one more toy's work, which is also the natural cost of having to collect and re-shelve it. `a_wrong_shelf_costs_about_one_toys_worth_of_time` pins the *relationship* rather than the constant, so a retune of shop size, walking speed or tools shows up instead of quietly making the penalty trivial or savage.
- **Decided: identity-derived detail, not 50×2 renderers.** A hundred new models is a great deal of work for an object the player sees for the length of one errand, and they would have to be kept in step with the fifty whole-toy renderers forever. Instead `toys/part_accents.rs` gives each identity a `PartAccent` — a crest shape and scale, a muzzle length, an eye spread — and the ten category renderers read it. Fifty table rows and one `draw_crest` helper. `no_two_identities_in_a_category_break_into_the_same_part` compares only the fields a category actually draws, so a copy-pasted row fails rather than silently shipping two identical halves.

## Game loop and progression

- **Run length is decided: 240 toys, 12 per display.** The complete earned-tool shift is 21.4 min: 240 toys shelved, all 28 repairs finished, all five tools bought, and 9 credits left. The capacity sweep below deliberately measures the sortable floor only, leaving broken pairs for the separate repair pass; its 27.9/17.6 figures are brackets, not complete shifts. `shop_scale_sets_the_length_of_a_shift` (`cargo test --release shop_scale -- --ignored --nocapture`) remains the thing to re-run before changing `toy_count`:

  |---|---|---|---|---|

  | **12** | **240** | **27.9 min** | **17.6 min** | **13** |
  | 20 | 400 | 46.8 min | 29.5 min | 14 |
  | 40 | 800 | 92.4 min | 62.1 min | 12 |
  | 100 | 2000 | 228.9 min | 174.0 min | 12 |
  | 200 | 4000 | 456.9 min | 363.5 min | 12 |

  Sortable-floor cost is ~7.8s per toy bare-handed at every size, so that phase scales linearly with `toy_count`. **Depth is no longer a constraint** — see the shelf-shadowing entry under Engineering; before that fix this table also carried a shelf-refusal column that went 2, 1133, 7173, 15793 as displays got deeper, and the two largest shops could not be filled at all. Capacity 12 now rests on the verified 21.4-minute complete route and the credit economy: the sorting pass completes enough displays to buy all five tools before the dedicated repair sweep.
- **The scanner now gives one honest route recommendation.** Four fixtures accept every category, so naming the first matching display while highlighting all four made the HUD and scene disagree. It now names and brightly marks the nearest compatible display with room, while the other valid fixtures retain a quieter outline. Filling the recommended display reroutes both text and highlight through the same `recommended_display_index` query.
- **Manager's Nod is a safety net, not an accounting discount.** Buying it grants twenty-five fresh guards regardless of earlier mistakes. A guarded wrong placement records the mistake for scoring but stops before the toy leaves the hand, occupies the slot, or adds the eight-second penalty. Old saves initialise only their plausibly unused guards once; current saves cannot refill them by reloading.
- **The nine surplus credits have a bounded late-shift outlet.** Once all five permanent tools are owned, a credit calls the Stockroom Spotlight for 60 seconds, stacking to 180. Its gold scene beacon and minimap marker identify the nearest loose toy but do not identify its shelf or move it, so it removes endgame pixel hunting without playing the sorting loop. Service spend and remaining duration survive save/load, and a call at capacity consumes nothing.
- **Deadline and relaxed mode are in.** `shift_seconds` (1800) ends a timed run at `GamePhase::TimeUp`; the title offers *Closing Shift* against the clock and *Relaxed Run* without one, `ShiftMode` persists in the save, and the HUD counts down (amber under 5 min, red under 1). The mistake penalty now bites, because pushing `elapsed_seconds` can end the shift outright — pinned by `state/tests/shift_clock.rs`.
- **Variety is seeded without moving the competitive target.** Closing Shift always uses `C105-1A6F-7EED-0001`, which derives zero variation and therefore preserves the exact scatter behind the 21.4-minute balance replay. Every new Relaxed Run mixes a fresh clock nonce into positions, tumble poses, colours, broken-toy selection, and cross-zone head scatter. The full 64-bit code is persisted and shown on pause/score screens; `F5` replays it while `R` asks for a new Relaxed scatter. Equal-seed sessions serialize byte-for-byte identically, three varied seeds keep all 240 bodies clear of fixtures with every repair pair split across zones, and old saves default to the fixed Closing seed.
- **The score screen is in** (`ui/score.rs`): grade badge, toys shelved, repairs, wrong shelves, time, and a per-aisle bar table, driven by `GameSession::shift_summary`. Finishing an aisle now announces itself mid-run via `InteractionResult::Placed.completed_zone`. The HUD hides behind the panel so the score is the whole message.
- **The ~88% zone cap is now legible rather than weakened.** `ZoneProgress::broken` counts an aisle's toys currently in halves and `still_to_find()` the rest of its shortfall, so the score screen reads "41 to find - 5 to mend" and the HUD "Plush Corner - 5 to mend / 90%". An aisle with toys in pieces is genuinely not restored, so `is_restored` was left alone; what was wrong was that a player who had shelved every whole toy in an aisle had no way to tell the remainder from toys they had missed. Pinned by `every_aisle_slot_is_accounted_for_as_shelved_broken_or_missing`.
- **A shift is now worth repeating: best runs are recorded.** The game graded you and forgot — nothing carried between shifts by design, since tools are earned and lost inside one run, so a player who cleared 92% had no way to know next time whether they had beaten it. `state/records.rs` keeps a `ShiftRecord` per mode in its own save slot (`records_slot`), separate from the session because "New Game" overwrites that one and a record has to survive exactly that. The score screen reads "Best so far: 154 toys, 2 wrong, 28:32", or "New best" when the run improved it.
  - Better means **more toys, then fewer wrong shelves, then faster** — in that order, so a careful slow clear beats a scrappy quick one and speed only settles a tie. Deliberately not the grade, which is a coarse band that two different runs can share.
  - The title screen shows the record too, above the buttons that pick a mode — by the time the score screen reports one the choice has already been made, and with nothing else carrying between shifts the record is the only reason to pick the clock. Both title captions now sit on a dark plate: 15px text unbacked over a lit shop full of bright toys was barely readable, which applied to the pre-existing mode caption as well.
  - **Records never actually loaded, and the tests all passed.** `load_from_slot_with_migration` hands its callback the whole save wrapper, `{slot, data}`, not the inner payload — and every field of `BestRuns` is `serde(default)`, so deserialising the wrapper straight into it did not fail. Serde ignored the two unknown keys and returned an empty record. Written correctly, silently read back as none, every launch. The fix unwraps the envelope first, exactly as `migrate_save_value` already did for the session save; that one was never affected. Found by `on_disk::a_record_survives_a_round_trip_through_a_file`, written specifically because the disk path was the one link reasoned about rather than observed. The other two on-disk tests cover a missing file (the ordinary first run) and a corrupt one, which must cost the player their records and nothing else.
  - Timed and Relaxed keep **separate** records. A relaxed run has no deadline, so its time is not comparable; pooling them would let an unhurried perfect clear stand as *the* record and leave a Closing Shift player nothing reachable to chase.
- **The deadline is reachable, and now proved by a finished shift.** `the_deadline_is_reachable_by_a_closer_who_buys_tools` drives one continuous `Strategy::Earner` session through the sortable floor, every cross-zone repair errand, the full tool ladder, and the final placement. It finishes 240/240 with 28 repairs in 21.4 min against the 30 min deadline: 8.6 min / 28.7% modelled headroom. The test asserts `Finished`, no usable toy left loose, every tool owned, and at least 15% headroom. This replaces the old 20.0-minute claim, which stopped at 213 shelved toys and 54 loose repair parts while calling that partial report a clear. A human playthrough is still worthwhile because the replay's straight-line routing and 0.6s interaction constant are intentionally idealised.

## Polish

- **The shop now answers without becoming noisy.** Ten deterministic procedural cues distinguish footsteps, pickup, drop, correct and wrong shelving, repair, tool/service purchase, display completion, closing warnings, and full restoration. The ordinary sorting cues stay under a quarter-second and every cue has a cooldown; a held movement key therefore produces a soft ~3 Hz footfall instead of one sound per frame. A quiet eight-second sine/triangle ambience bed starts with the shift and stops at the title.
- **Audio uses the shared toolkit synthesis and settings paths, with a deliberately thin game-specific player.** WAV bytes are rendered at startup from `macroquad_toolkit::synth`, decoded independently so one failed cue cannot remove the rest, and the capture/performance harness uses a silent director. The toolkit's persisted master, effects, and music fields are exposed as Master, Effects, and Ambience; master multiplies both channels, 0% is real silence, and changing Effects previews the pickup cue. No binary asset, manifest entry, or new dependency was added.
- **The first shift now teaches itself in play.** A six-step guide starts with look/move, then pickup and correct category shelving. Repair, tool-rack, and trolley lessons stay silent until the player is carrying a broken part, has earned a tool, or has multiple trolley toys respectively, so an instruction never names a mechanic the player cannot use yet. The live bottom prompt remains authoritative for the exact `E` action; the guide explains the larger task. `H` marks it learned for experienced players, and Settings can replay it.
- **Help and accessibility are no longer README-only.** Controls & How to Play is a full in-game panel with the complete key map and closing routine. Toybox-specific preferences persist FOV, mouse sensitivity, contrast, and tutorial completion independently of the resumable shift; the toolkit settings store persists fullscreen and UI text size. High contrast deepens surfaces and lifts parchment/brass edges, while the 90–120% text scale is multiplied into resolution-aware scaling rather than being overwritten each frame.
- **The title screen's craft now carries through the whole shift.** The flat black gameplay slabs have been replaced by one code-native material vocabulary: walnut surfaces, parchment type, brass edges and pins, warm keycaps, and state colour used as information rather than decoration. The tall 250×226 status slab is now a 594×104 shelf-like header, keeping more of the shop visible while placing clock, total progress, trolley load, and current-aisle progress on one scan line. `TIME LEFT` / `TIME ELAPSED` keeps the two clock directions explicit.
- **The trolley and directory now read as objects, not debug panes.** The carried card has room for an untruncated identity, Q-cycle tokens, and a separate drop key; the minimap sits inside a labelled Store Directory frame with the live home count. The context prompt uses the same warm surface and keycaps, so the three bottom-edge elements feel composed even when all are visible together.
- **The tool rack and closing report now have real hierarchy.** Tool states use a left rail, unlock medallion, plain status text, and distinct Owned / Locked / Need credit / Buy controls; the all-tools service retains its own gold card. The score screen promotes outcome and grade into a header/medallion, turns the four summary values into cards, and leaves aisle rows visually quieter. Both win and loss captures remain readable over the shop instead of resembling an equally weighted spreadsheet.

- **Screenshot captures were not reproducible, which is why staleness could only ever be caught by eye.** The harness simulates a fixed number of frames at a fixed timestep precisely so a capture repeats — but four `get_time()` calls (a lamp breathing, dust motes, completion sparkles, the scanner beacon) read macroquad's wall clock, so the animation phase depended on how long the process took to reach the capture frame. The same scene captured twice differed in bytes, so no drift check could ever tell a stale reference image from a fresh one. `ui::animation_seconds()` now accumulates the same `dt` the simulation gets; `rendering_animates_off_the_simulation_clock` keeps the wall clock out of `src/ui` and `src/toys`.
  - Seven of nine scenes went from varying to byte-identical across repeated captures. The residual wobble is confined to anti-aliased text edges in the HUD panel, with channel deltas up to 39/255. Nothing in this repo reads a clock any more, the spatial grid sorts so draw order is fixed, and raising the capture from 30 to 150 frames does not settle it — so it is not animation phase and not a framebuffer settle-in. It looks like font-atlas rasterisation in the shared toolkit; still unproven, but no longer in the way.
  - **Re-measured: the two named scenes are not the unstable ones any more.** `mid_run` and `carrying_a_half_scanned` are now byte-identical across repeated captures (three runs, 0 pixels). The one that still moves is **`carrying_armful`** — 2–29 pixels typically, once ~300 (0.035%) — at two spots, the status-panel clock and the prompt row. Worth knowing before trusting any per-scene claim here: which scene wobbles has changed at least once without anyone noticing.
- **Capture drift is detectable now: `.\scripts\check-captures.ps1`.** It re-captures all 76 references — the 26 scenes *and* the 50-toy gallery whose staleness started this — into gitignored `dist\capture-check`, compares against the committed images without overwriting them, and exits non-zero on drift. A hash cannot work for the scenes because of the wobble above, so it measures the **percentage of differing pixels** — now counted only past a channel delta, see below. A genuinely stale reference measured **3.159%**; the check caught one on its first run, an image accidentally left behind from a 150-frame experiment.
  - **No any-pixel threshold could separate scene noise from a real change, and the 0.1% gate proved it by missing one.** The wobble reaches 0.035% at its worst; the `LEFT`/`ELAPSED` caption that slipped through measured 0.019%. The signal was *smaller than the noise*, so tightening the number was never going to work.
  - **Channel contrast separates them cleanly.** Over five repeat captures the noise never exceeds a channel delta of 39 and is flatly **zero** above 64, while a real HUD text change (`mid_run` vs `relaxed_run`, differing only in the clock digits and the caption) puts **838 pixels** past delta 64. `compare_captures.py --min-delta 64` counts only those, which drops every scene's noise floor to 0.000% and lets the scene threshold come down to **0.01%**.
    - Verified both directions on the exact class that escaped: a synthetic change touching only the caption cell (370 px) **passes** the old gate at 0.043% and **fails** the new one at 0.017%. All 26 scenes read 0.000% under it.
    - An earlier version of `compare_captures.py` said the 39-delta noise "rules out a delta tolerance". That was reasoned from the noise alone without ever measuring the signal, which sits an order of magnitude above it — the same shape of mistake as the crosshair numbers, and the reason the gate stayed too loose for so long.
  - The toy gallery deliberately keeps **no** contrast gate (any differing pixel counts) at 0.01%. ## Engineering

- **`worst_frame_ms` could not report a number worse than 100.00, so the probe said nothing about stutter.** It was measured from the `dt` handed to `update`, which `main.rs` clamps to 0.1s so one long frame cannot teleport the simulation — a correct clamp for the *simulation*, silently wrong as a *measurement*. A two-second stall and a 100ms hitch read identically. The bench now takes the unclamped `get_frame_time()`, skips the first 60 frames (shader compilation and first-draw costs are startup, not stutter — and one 90ms opening frame otherwise set the figure for the whole run and hid everything after it), and counts `slow_frames` over 16.7ms alongside the worst.
  - **This was the check the LOD change needed and could not get.** Raising `toy_lod_distance` 5 → 8 draws far more geometry, which is exactly the change that could introduce hitches. A/B over four runs each: `slow_frames` is **4–5 per 10 seconds at both settings**, and the worst frame is 34–42ms at both. The extra geometry costs average throughput and nothing else — the hitches are independent of render load.
  - What those hitches *are* is unattributed. Across 5/20/30-second runs the count went 3/6/16, which is neither flat nor cleanly linear, and this machine was compiling throughout. It is a baseline to compare against on a quiet machine, not a verdict — and specifically **not** evidence of a game-internal periodic stall, which is what I would have concluded from the 10-second runs alone.
- **Every toy past five metres was a coloured cube, and the middle LOD band was dead code.** `draw_loose_toys` picks a stand-in cube beyond `toy_lod_distance`, a full *upright* model beyond `toy_pose_distance`, and a full posed model closer than that — but the middle arm is an `else if`, and both distances were **5.0**. Setting them equal deletes that band silently, so the shop rendered as posed models within 5m and boxes everywhere else. The LOD was tuned when the shop held 4000 toys; it holds 240 now, and nothing revisited it after that retune. `toy_lod_distance` is now **8.0**, which makes the upright band live and turns the mid-field from a scatter of boxes into recognisable dragons, bears and blocks.
  - **The perf probe could not have caught this, or anything else.** `TOYBOX_BENCH_SECONDS` reported 59.5 fps on every run because it was vsync-capped — it was measuring the monitor, so a regression would only show once the game was *already* dropping frames. `window_conf` now sets `swap_interval = 0` for a bench run only (the earliest hook there is, hence `game::bench_seconds()` being read from both places). Uncapped, the shipped shop measures ~320 fps.
  - Swept the distance against frame cost on the 240-toy shop: lod 5 → ~320 fps, 7 → 272, 8 → 245, 9 → 221, 12 (no cubes at all) → 113. 12 is the one I did **not** take, because WebGL is the primary target and several times slower. Still unmeasured — the extension connects now but cannot reach this host's loopback (see Validation), so a browser frame-rate number is what this decision is waiting on.
  - **Correction: that sweep was single samples, and it understated the cost.** Four runs per setting under the same conditions give lod 5 → 262–346 fps (mean 316) and lod 8 → 166–219 (mean 200): **1.6×**, not the 1.3× first reported. Still an easy trade at 5ms a frame natively, and it does not change the decision, but run-to-run variance on this machine is ±30% and one sample per point cannot see it. Take a mean of several runs before trusting a bench delta.
  - `every_toy_render_band_is_reachable` pins the ordering — lod strictly beyond pose, render strictly beyond lod — so equal values fail rather than quietly deleting a band again.
  - **The drift check earned its keep, as a backstop rather than an inventory.** I re-captured the fourteen scenes I judged to draw loose toys and missed `tool_shop`/`tool_shop_early`, which render the shop behind their panel; the check flagged both at ~0.6%. This is the same lesson as the clock caption, from the other side: it will not tell you *what* changed, but it does catch what you forgot. Also ran the full 76 — scenes and the 50-toy gallery — for the first time in several rounds; the gallery is unaffected, since it draws through `gallery.rs` rather than the scene LOD.
- Balance the five tools against measured pacing. Over the sortable-floor pass: bare-handed 27.9 min, trolley+scanner 20.5, fully equipped 17.6 — so a full toolset cuts that phase by about 37%. The trolley now clearly pays (it used to measure *worse* than bare hands); the speed tools stack on top. The complete earned-tool route is the separate 21.4-minute result above, including every repair.
- The replay closer used to walk every toy to the **first** display of its category, ignoring the other three. That fills a quarter of the shop and then spins, carrying each remaining toy to a shelf that has been full for hours and dropping it again — 9700 wasted actions in an 8000-action shift. Every "the shop is unfinishable" number before this was measuring that, not the game. `home_display_index` now picks the nearest matching display *with room*, and `the_closer_clears_the_floor_without_misshelving` asserts the run ends because it ran out of work rather than out of budget.
- **Placement now runs through real aiming** (`aim_and_place`): the closer walks to a gap, faces it, and presses `E` only once `interaction_preview` says it will shelve — so a run is charged for putting each toy away, not just for finding it. With a trolley that is a separate walk per toy in the armful, because the slot just filled is no longer the one the crosshair offers. Doing this uncovered two `interact` ordering bugs and the shelf shadowing below.
- **A full front row used to shadow every row behind it.** Slots sit five to a row, so a display's capacity is its depth, and `targeted_empty_display_slot` took the best-scoring slot and then asked whether it happened to be free. Scoring favours the nearest, so once row one filled, standing at the shelf offered a taken slot and `E` refused with two empty rows a few centimetres further back. It now searches free slots only — holding a toy, the intent is unambiguous, so an occupied slot is not a candidate. Bare-handed shelf refusals went to zero at every capacity, and the 4000-toy shop went from 146 toys shelved and 0 displays completed to 3521 and 12. `a_full_front_row_does_not_block_the_rows_behind_it` pins the rule; `a_display_stays_fillable_to_its_back_row` keeps watching the shipped config.
- The "crosshair misses half the time" reading was **two different things added together**, and the report now splits them:
  - *Whiffed* — the crosshair delivered nothing. Bare-handed this is **zero**, and re-measuring confirms it holds at every shop size from 160 to 4000 toys; the cone is fine. What produced the original 175 whiffs was the Sorting Trolley having no input path at all (fixed: `E` on a loose toy now loads the armful when there is room).
  - *Neighbour* — the crosshair handed over a different toy than the one aimed at. The closer still walks away holding a toy, so this is pile texture rather than lost work.
  - **Both figures were an order of magnitude stale, and the reasoning attached to them had quietly stopped applying.** "~175–195 of 400" and "arguably the point of a floor buried in toys" were measured when the shop held 4000 toys. It ships 240. Re-measured across the capacity sweep, neighbour grabs as a share of toys shelved:

    | toys | bare-handed | fully equipped |
    |---|---|---|
    | 160 | 0% | 4.2% |
    | **240 (shipped)** | **1.9%** | **6.6%** |
    | 400 | 1.1% | 12.9% |
    | 800 | 4.9% | 26.2% |
    | 2000 | 20.3% | 82.2% |
    | 4000 | 19.6% | **99.0%** |

    So the old number was not wrong, it was measured on a shop sixteen times denser than the one that ships — where a fully equipped closer effectively never gets the toy it aimed at. At 240 toys the floor is not buried and the phenomenon is a handful of grabs per run. Nothing to fix; the decision to leave it stands, but for the opposite reason to the one recorded.
  - `the_crosshair_hands_over_the_toy_you_aimed_at` now guards both rates so they cannot go stale in prose again: whiffs under 5% and neighbours under 20% of toys shelved. The gates sit ~3× above the shipped rates and below the pathological end — the same closer crosses the neighbour gate at 800 toys (26.2%) and the whiff gate at 4000 (7.0%), so density creeping back up fails a test instead of quietly changing what the game feels like.
  - The sweep also re-confirms two neighbouring entries at every density: `noslot` (turned away from a shelf) is **0 bare-handed at all six shop sizes**, so the shelf-shadowing fix holds well past the size it was found at.
